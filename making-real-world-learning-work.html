A proposal for university learning

Making Real-World Learning Work

A proposal to help universities support student teams working across disciplines on real problems with community partners.

Christopher Rogers

01 Work that matters

Imagine engineering, public health, design, and policy students investigating community water quality. With a community partner, they test a prototype and improve it. Faculty guidance and academic credit connect this example to what students are learning.

02 What makes it possible

My exchange with Bass Connections director Meghan O’Neil highlighted the support these teams need: connecting people, making participation workable, and helping teams from proposal to completion.1 Universities need a shared process for providing that support.

To reach more students, universities need
a repeatable way to support teams.

Putting the proposal into practice

Make participation workable.

Start with a small project in one university, then use what it teaches you to improve the support.

04 Start within one university

  1. Choose a small pilot. Bring a few disciplines together around a partner-defined challenge with an achievable outcome.
  2. Agree before recruiting. Set credit, assessment, faculty workload, funding, and decision ownership.
  3. Give coordination a home. Assign program support and a team project manager with clear responsibilities.
  4. Learn before expanding. Track setup time, faculty effort, student learning, and value to partners. Adjust the approach.
A longer-term ambition: extend collaboration across universities once the approach works internally. That would require agreements on credit, calendars, compensation, and decision-making.

05 Support the whole experience

Bass Connections provides shared resources for team goals, charters, collaboration, and reflection.2 Its project-management guidance calls for clear responsibilities and compensation, alongside continued faculty leadership and mentorship.3

What O’Neil’s account adds

  • A central team works with leaders in each program theme.
  • Support extends from proposal development through the showcase.
  • Leading a team usually does not count for faculty teaching credit.1

Design for the people doing the work

Faculty need time and support to lead collaborative teams. Students need clear expectations and feedback. Partners need a voice in defining useful work.

What to test nextCan a shared support process make it easier for more students, faculty, and partners to take part?

Introduction

Started in October 2026, this proposal explores how universities can help students earn academic credit through projects across disciplines. My exchange with Bass Connections director Meghan O’Neil informed the five decisions and project cycle. Written for university leaders, faculty, and program staff, it is a work in progress focused on making collaboration easier to organize and sustain.

Reflection

Developing this proposal shifted my attention from the benefits of real-world learning to the support it requires. Projects need faculty time, academic credit, coordination, and reliable resources. My exchange with Meghan O’Neil made that work visible. I began thinking about how university processes could make participation easier for students and community partners.
